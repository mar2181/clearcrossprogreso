If you have never crossed at Progreso before, the trip is shorter and plainer than most people expect. You park in Texas, you walk over a bridge, and the shops and clinics start almost immediately.

The parts that go wrong are rarely dramatic. They are small: the wrong document, no small change, no written quote, no idea how long the queue home will be.

This is the list.

## Before you leave the house

**Documents.** A **valid passport or passport card**. A driver's licence or a state ID will not get you back into the United States, and this is the single most common way a trip ends before it starts. Check the expiry date while you are looking at it.

**Cash.** US dollars are widely accepted on the tourist strip. Bring a backup card, but do not plan around every counter taking one.

**Quarters.** The turnstile walking into Mexico takes four of them, per person. Coming back is cheaper and can usually be paid in pesos.

**The clinic's details written down** — name, street address, phone number — not only in an app on a phone that might not have signal.

**Tell somebody.** Where you are going, which clinic, and roughly when you expect to be back.

## Have this settled before you arrive

**Know roughly what the work should cost.** We publish the prices each clinic has put out publicly, researched from published price lists rather than supplied or confirmed by the clinic. That gives you a range to walk in with. It is not a quote, and it is not a promise about what you will be charged.

**Ask for the quote in writing, before anything starts.** Itemised: what is being done, what each part costs, what happens if more is needed once they are looking. A verbal number in a waiting room is not something you can hold anybody to.

**Know what happens if something goes wrong at home.** Ask before treatment, not after: if this needs adjusting in three weeks, what does that look like, and what does it cost? You will be four hours away in Weslaco or McAllen, not down the road.

## Questions worth asking at the clinic

None of these are rude. They are the ordinary questions any careful patient asks, and a clinic that takes them badly has told you something useful.

- **"May I see your Cédula Profesional?"** Mexican dentists and doctors are required to hold one, and it is normal for it to be displayed. Nobody from ClearCross has inspected a clinic or checked anybody's licence, so this is a question for you to ask in the room.
- **"How many of these do you do in a month?"** Volume is a fair proxy for familiarity.
- **"How do you sterilise instruments here?"** You are allowed to ask, and you are allowed to look.
- **"What are you using?"** For a crown, an implant, a lens — ask what the brand and material actually are, and write the answer down. We have not looked in anybody's cupboard and cannot tell you what a given clinic stocks.
- **"What is the total, in writing, before we begin?"**

## On the day

**Go early.** The town is busiest in the middle of the day. Mornings are calmer, and weekdays are calmer than weekends.

**Keep every receipt.** For the treatment, for the pharmacy, for anything you buy. You will want them at customs and you will want them if there is ever a dispute.

**Photograph the work.** Before and after, on your own phone. It costs nothing and it is the only record you will control.

**Drink bottled water.**

## Coming back

The queue you should plan around is the one going *home* — that is where everyone is inspected.

Check the live wait rather than assuming today is like last time; it moves hour to hour. We show the current CBP reading for the Progreso crossing on our [safety and crossing page](/safety), with CBP's own timestamp on it.

Declare what you bought. Each person gets an $800 exemption every 30 days, and declaring is what keeps a bag of shopping boring. There is a full breakdown in [what you can legally bring back](/blog/what-you-can-bring-back-from-nuevo-progreso), including the rules for medication.

## The short version

If you only remember five things:

1. Passport or passport card, in date.
2. Quarters, and cash.
3. A written, itemised quote before any work starts.
4. Ask to see the Cédula.
5. Check the live border wait before you walk back.

Everything else is shopping.
